## COPY RULES (Sabri Suby $100M Checklist — apply to every word)

- 4th–5th grade reading level. Simple words. No jargon.
- Short, punchy sentences. Mix lengths for rhythm.
- Every line answers one of: Why care? Why believe? Why now?
- Every line pulls into the next. No dead ends.
- Write to ONE person (Bryan). Never "Hey guys."
- Stories and mental pictures over facts alone.
- Cut ruthlessly. Every word earns its place.
- Consumption precedes conversion. If they can't read it fast, they won't buy.
- **Clarity beats cleverness.** If a line can be said simpler, say it simpler. A pun that costs one second of comprehension is a net loss — consumption precedes conversion.
- **"One person" in Taglish means `ikaw` / `mo` — never `kayo` / `ninyo` / `po kayo`.** Taglish politeness defaults to the plural-formal, which silently breaks the write-to-one-person rule while every English-language check still passes. This is the most common way the rule above gets violated without anyone noticing.
- **Readability test for Taglish assets is the SPOKEN test, not a grade level.** "4th–5th grade" is an English-calibrated metric (Flesch-Kincaid et al.) and does not measure Taglish — an agent "hitting 4th grade" in Taglish is reporting a number that means nothing. Use instead: *would a 45-year-old sari-sari store owner say this sentence out loud, to a friend, without stumbling?* If no, rewrite. Keep the literal 4th–5th grade target for the English-language surfaces below, where it does measure.
- **Outcome before feature.** Describe what the customer GETS, never what the engine DOES. "Replaces your marketing agency" beats "40 workflows, 15 reports, 300 captions." Feature counts are technology-first; outcome paragraphs are customer-first. At the buy moment especially — never feature-list.

## AWARENESS LADDER (Schwartz's 5 Levels — adopted 2026-07-20, Neil-approved)

Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966). Every asset targets ONE stage —
name it in your reasoning like a Cialdini principle:

1. **Unaware** — lead with story/symptom they recognize; never the brand, never the product.
2. **Problem Aware** — name the pain sharper than they can; agitate, then bridge.
3. **Solution Aware** — differentiate the mechanism; kill the category objection (scam-fear, "AI garbage").
4. **Product Aware** — proof, process transparency, offer stack, objection handling.
5. **Most Aware** — the deal: CTA, urgency, terms. Shortest copy.

Rules of use:
- **The hook must match the viewer's stage, not ours.** Product-speak at an Unaware
  audience is the #1 mismatch (e.g. "certified siphoning kami" to someone who doesn't
  know DENR liability exists — lead with the fine/risk instead).
- **Tag assets** (`awareness` field in script pools, ledger entries) so winners
  correlate by stage. Batch mix: never ship a week of content all on one stage.
- **Emergency-purchase caveat (Oceanides):** emergency buyers teleport Unaware →
  desperate-Problem-Aware overnight; low-stage content is a RECALL play, not a
  converter. It must never become the excuse for vanity metrics — attributed
  calls/bookings stay the scoreboard (Gate 2).
- **Companion lens: market sophistication** (same book). In claim-exhausted markets
  (malabanan "mura! mabilis!"), don't shout louder — switch to a new mechanism or
  identity claim (SEC/DENR papers, Kuya Marvin himself).

## THUMBNAIL RULES (decoded MrBeast frame grammar)

**Canonical spec:** [`../_shared/thumbnail-law.md`](../_shared/thumbnail-law.md) — decoded
2026-07-28 from 15 real MrBeast thumbnails (all examined). Re-read it before briefing any
thumbnail, ad still, or hero frame. It wins on any conflict with this summary.

**THE LAW:** text in the frame is never a message. It is a **number** that quantifies the stake, a
**label physically present in the scene**, or the **word being spoken** at that instant. Never a
sentence written to the viewer. Zero of the 15 carry a marketing sentence — if you need one, the
image failed; fix the image.

*(This replaces the old "3–5 big words overlaid" rule, which all 15 files contradict — it described
the 2019–2021 MrBeast era. Don't reintroduce it.)*

- **One focal point.** Face left-third or center, foreground, rim-lit; "the thing" on the other side.
- **Contrast device** — two poles in one frame that shouldn't coexist ($1 vs $1B, baby vs elderly).
  This is the Purple Cow made visual. Name the second pole before briefing the visual.
- **Expression defaults to a warm open smile**, not shock. Escalate only when the premise earns it —
  forced shock reads as AI slop.
- **Prize numbers yes, price numbers never.** A number for what they GET (outcome, scale, count,
  duration) is the engine. A peso price, tier, or discount stays banned in thumbnails and scripts.
- When text IS legitimately used on a Grifon-branded still, it follows brand: `#FFCC00` + white on
  dark. Never on the face.
- **Paid ads: S12 outranks MrBeast.** `S12 pattern-interrupt`, 9 ads / 5 brands, is our own
  DR-gated evidence. MrBeast's numbers came from free content, where a wrong click costs 3 seconds
  instead of ad spend — pure curiosity gap on a paid ad trains Andromeda on clickers, not buyers.
