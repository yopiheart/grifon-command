You already use
liquid glass.
On the wrong layer.

Apple's whole thesis is that glass belongs to the functional layer — navigation and controls floating above content — and explicitly not to the content layer itself. Grifon does the exact opposite: GlassPanel wraps every form, every card, every data display. The chrome that should be glass — headers, rails, step footers — is mostly flat.

So the answer is yes, it's worth applying. Not as a coat of paint. As an inversion.

And one thing no count will tell you

Glass over zinc-950 is optically a no-op. Refraction needs something behind it worth bending. On a near-black canvas with nothing but a static gold radial, backdrop-blur-xl costs a GPU pass and buys a faint grey wash — which matters on the mid-range Android hardware your Filipino SMB users actually run. Apple's glass sits over photos, video, and scrolling content. Yours mostly sits over a colour.

That's the unlock in the stage above: the pane only looks expensive because there's moving footage beneath it. Grifon has exactly that footage — it's the product's own output. Put it behind the chrome and the glass finally earns its cost.

Six moves, in order

01
✓ Shipped — GlassPanel now PLATE

Invert the layers — glass goes to chrome, content goes tonal

Demote GlassPanel to a PLATE: tonal fill, no backdrop-filter, hairline top-light. Promote the true chrome — SiteHeader, DashboardSidebar, StepFooter, CommandPalette, PinnedCtaPill, toolbars — to CHROME. One primitive swap, ~90 fewer compositing layers, and the hierarchy finally reads: what floats is what you can operate.

Put something behind the glass

The chrome layer needs content passing beneath it to mean anything. Extend the app's own generated frames, the ad montage, and the Aurora field under the floating bars instead of stopping at their edge — Apple calls this the background extension effect. This is the single move that takes Grifon from "dark SaaS with blur" to "cinema glass," and it costs almost nothing because the footage already exists.

03
✓ Shipped — .mat-chrome

Upgrade the material itself — edge lensing + specular rim

Today's glass is blur + 80% tint + 1px white border. Real glass refracts hardest at the bevel: a masked rim band with its own heavier blur and saturation, a bright inset top-light, a dimmer bottom-light, and a specular that travels on hover. Pure CSS, no assets, no library. That's the difference between "frosted div" and "machined pane" — compare the two states in the stage above.

04
✓ Shipped — both guards live

Ship the accessibility guards you're missing

Add prefers-reduced-transparency and prefers-contrast: more fallbacks beside the existing reduced-motion blocks in index.css: glass collapses to a solid tonal fill, rim band off, contrast ring on. Apple treats this as non-optional — and it's the one item here that's a genuine defect rather than a taste call. Smallest diff on the list.

05
✓ Shipped — SiteHeader

Scroll edge effect under every sticky bar

Content currently slides under the pricing sticky nav and dashboard top bar with only a blur to protect it, so labels collide with whatever passes beneath. Apple's fix is a graded fade at the bar's edge that dims and blurs the incoming content — a masked gradient, ~15 lines. Legibility stops depending on what happens to be scrolling.

06
Helper only — no call sites yet

Make radii concentric, not merely consistent

You already have three tiers and a 44px control floor in wizardTokens.ts — ahead of most teams. The missing rule is nesting: an inner radius should equal the outer radius minus the padding between them, so curves stay parallel instead of drifting. A 16px panel with 12px padding wants a 4px inner corner, not another rounded-xl. Invisible individually; the whole app tightens at once.

// Straight answer: where this does not belong

Five places to not do it

✕ The landing hero

Liquid Glass is a deference material — it recedes so content can lead. A marketing hero's job is to assert. Copying Apple here would sand off the cinematic identity that's doing the selling. Keep the hero loud; glass the nav that floats over it.

✕ The proof band

#F2F1EA cards are solid by documented rule, and that rule is right — the band's whole job is "printed page," the opposite register. Apple would agree: no glass in the content layer.

✕ Generated ad output

Nothing here touches the render pipeline, prompts, or creative templates. UI chrome only. A glass look inside a client's ad is a 2020 trend, not a 2050 one.

✕ Dense wizard forms

Steps 1–7 are long, field-heavy, and read top-to-bottom. Translucency behind small text is a legibility tax with no upside. Plate them; glass only the sticky step footer that floats over them.

✕ Anything relying on SwiftUI APIs

glassEffect, GlassEffectContainer, and concentric-rectangle APIs are free on Apple platforms and absent on the web. Everything above is hand-built CSS — budget for that, and don't expect the system to adapt tint or luminosity for you.

✕ As a reason to add more blur

If a change ends with a higher backdrop-blur count, it's the wrong change. The target is 105 → ~17, with the survivors doing visibly more work.

// Bottom line

It was worth it — as a demotion, not an addition.

Apple's material is a hierarchy argument wearing a visual-effect costume. Take the argument, leave the costume. Grifon's identity — zinc-950 canvas, one gold accent, cinematic type — survives all six moves untouched. What changes is that glass becomes rare enough to mean something.
