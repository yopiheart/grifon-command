# Reference evaluation: Grifon companion

Reference: [OpenAI’s Head of ChatGPT: We’re entering a new era of AI (again) | Tibo Sottiaux](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MM-C3JqCXBk), published by Lenny’s Podcast. This is an interview with an OpenAI leader, rather than an official OpenAI product demonstration. [Publisher’s episode page](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/openais-head-of-chatgpt-were-entering).

The full public, auto-generated English captions were inspected. The timestamps below are approximate and the takeaways are paraphrases; captions can contain recognition errors.

## Judgment

Worth applying to Grifon’s interaction model now. The strongest lesson is to reduce the work users do to configure an agent: let them state their goal, then expose only the choices, progress and approvals that matter. It does not prove that a conversational interface alone can deliver finished ads or run reliable background work.

## What changes the current interface

- [3:34–4:00: goals and preferences](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MM-C3JqCXBk&t=214s). Keep task entry conversational. Grifon should collect missing information rather than ask users to assemble the agent workflow. Natural intake and preference learning still need their own backend milestone.
- [7:39–8:20: reducing mode/configuration choices](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MM-C3JqCXBk&t=459s). Make chat the ordinary entry point and keep Studio available for explicit tool use. Preserve direct tool links and checkout intent instead of forcing every arrival through chat.
- [10:30–11:35: a primary companion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MM-C3JqCXBk&t=630s). Start with one Grifon and separate project conversations. A specialist roster adds a selection decision before the user can ask for work; it can wait until there is a demonstrated need.
- [17:50–20:07: attention and configuration fatigue](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MM-C3JqCXBk&t=1070s). Keep progress compact, outputs inspectable, and Settings limited to controls that actually work. Developer fixtures and inert notification toggles do not belong in the ordinary chat experience.

## What belongs to a later system milestone

[21:47–22:34: harness and guardrails](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MM-C3JqCXBk&t=1307s) is relevant to tool execution, task permissions, durable jobs and cross-device continuity. Those require runtime enforcement and verified recovery, not just a Working badge or an animated mascot.

General preferences separated from each project’s brief/files/decisions are Grifon’s approved architecture direction. This is our recommendation, not a claim about the internal storage architecture of Muse, Dots or Grok Bot.

## Additional first-party design references

- [Muse design account](https://introducing.muse.ai/): conversation, separate contexts, rich artifacts and approvals.
- [Grok Bot settings and notifications](https://docs.x.ai/grok-bot/settings-and-notifications): account/settings access and activity visibility.
- [Getting started with your dot](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001530-getting-started-with-your-dot): primary agent/profile and activity.

The useful adaptation is simple navigation with honest state. Copying their exact mascot, colors or agent list would not establish Grifon’s reliability or brand fit.
