# Reusable water bottle — directing brief v1

A 12-second vertical studio commercial: move closer, travel around the bottle, slide across it, then settle into a still hero portrait. The bottle stays upright and stationary, making each requested camera move easy to judge.

This is the 3D directing plan only. No scene, preview, appearance reference or finished video has been generated.

## Target and assumptions

- Required: reusable water bottle; exactly 12 seconds; vertical framing; push-in, orbit and side slide before the hero shot; no invented product claims.
- Proposed format: 9:16, with a 24 fps planning timeline (288 frames). Final resolution is not selected; 1080 × 1920 is a proposed delivery target, not a generated asset.
- Proposed final look: restrained photographic studio lighting on a quiet seamless background. The gray geometry used for a future directing preview would describe staging, not dictate the finished bottle's material or color.
- Audience and placement are unspecified. Assume a general product introduction suited to a vertical feed; no demographic or sales offer is inferred.
- No people, wardrobe, dialogue, handling or drinking. No claim copy, logo, CTA, liquid effects or demonstrations of insulation, leak resistance, durability or environmental benefit.

## Asset inventory and continuity

The only supplied source is the text brief. No product photo, dimensions, brand, character identity, location reference, music or reference video was supplied or inspected.

For a future preview, use an explicitly provisional bottle proxy: a simple body and closed-cap silhouette. Its proportions and cap geometry are placeholders, not assertions about the actual product. Define bottle height as H, base at the world origin, upright on Z, with +Y as the provisional front and +X as screen-right from the initial camera. A flat ground plane provides visible base contact; a seamless background avoids distracting scenery. There are no cast bindings.

Keep the bottle's pose, scale, orientation, closed-cap state and lighting fixed throughout. Move the camera rather than spinning the bottle. Actual shape, finish, color, branding and cap details remain unknown and must come from an approved appearance reference before a faithful product render or generation handoff.

## Shot plan

Times are contiguous; frame ranges are zero-based and inclusive. The lens starts with a consistent 50 mm perspective as a staging choice, adjusted only during preview review if necessary to preserve the intended framing.

| Shot | Time / frames | Purpose and visible action | Framing and camera direction | Cut, continuity and sound intent |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S01 — Push-in | 0.00–3.00 s / 0–71 | Introduce the complete bottle, then bring attention to its silhouette. The bottle remains still. | Front view, centered, camera aimed near mid-height. Dolly straight toward the bottle on the front axis; do not simulate this with a zoom. Begin with bottle occupying about 50% of picture height, finish near 70%, keeping cap and base visible. Ease into and out of the move. | Start directly on the product. Soft ambient music may begin here; no VO. Hard cut at 3.00 s. Ground contact and closed cap remain unchanged. |
| S02 — Orbit | 3.00–6.00 s / 72–143 | Reveal the bottle's volume through a clearly changing viewpoint. | Start at the preceding front-view scale. Move on a 60° arc from front toward the bottle's right side, at constant radius and camera height. Track the bottle center, keeping the full silhouette near 70% of frame height. The camera travels; the bottle does not rotate. | Match the previous shot's size at the cut. No lid action or new feature reveal is implied. Maintain the same world lighting so highlights change because of viewpoint. A restrained musical pulse can carry the motion. Hard cut at 6.00 s. |
| S03 — Side slide | 6.00–9.00 s / 144–215 | Create a clean lateral parallax beat before the final reveal. | Return by editorial cut to a frontal setup. Translate the camera left to right on a straight path parallel to the front plane, roughly from X = −0.6H to +0.6H at a fixed distance and height. Gently track the center to keep the bottle readable. Use the ground shadow/background falloff to reveal lateral travel. Keep cap and base inside frame. This is a linear slide, not another orbit. | The viewpoint reset is an intentional cut, not an implied physical camera move connecting shots. Bottle pose and lighting stay fixed. No contact, handoff or hidden product action occurs. Let the music lead into a resolving beat at 9.00 s. |
| S04 — Hero hold | 9.00–12.00 s / 216–287 | Resolve into a clear, memorable full-product portrait. | Cut to a balanced front three-quarter view, approximately 20° off the provisional front. Bottle centered, about 65–70% of frame height, with breathing room above and below. Lock the camera for all three seconds. No float, spin or continuing push. | Same bottle, closed cap and ground contact. No text or logo is invented. Music resolves softly and tails out by 12.00 s; no unmotivated cap, pouring or drinking SFX. |

**Timing check:** 3 + 3 + 3 + 3 = 12 seconds. All three requested moves precede the held hero shot. The visual story progresses from recognition to volume to lateral perspective, then resolves with time to inspect the product. “Reusable water bottle” comes from the brief; the sequence makes no additional performance claim.

## What to judge in the first 3D preview

The next review stage, if requested, would be a custom-authored local scene with editable camera paths, a labeled review preview, storyboard frames and a separate clean camera guide. Existing product pilots provide patterns for authoring and checking a scene; their fixed builders and exhausted paid runners are not reusable commands for this bottle job.

Success means a 12-second 9:16 sequence in the stated order, three visibly distinct camera moves, readable full-product framing and a genuinely still three-second hero hold. The proxy must stay planted on the ground with stable shape and pose. There should be no added branding, claims or product actions.

Failure would include a zoom replacing the push-in, a turntable replacing camera travel, a curved slide path, cropped cap/base, changing geometry, unintended floating, or drift during the hero hold. These are future preview checks, not tests already passed. Full-motion viewing plus duration, aspect, cut-boundary and decode checks would verify the eventual guide; still frames alone cannot establish smooth movement.
