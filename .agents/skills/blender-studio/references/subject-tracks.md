# Subject tracks

These are execution procedures and review contracts. Only the bundled Grifon
character has the accepted specimen and replay evidence in this skill. New
subjects require their own actual render and review; do not claim generalized
quality or automatic reconstruction from the character success.

## Shared production loop

1. Inspect the supplied references. List known geometry, materials, scale, and
   camera information. Label hidden sides or missing measurements as assumptions.
2. Set output requirements from the task: still, animation, interactive asset,
   resolution/aspect, transparency, target engine, and device where applicable.
3. Block out in real units where known. Inspect silhouette from at least front
   and three-quarter views before surface detail; use orthographic plan/elevation
   views when dimensions matter.
4. Build coherent editable geometry, then materials, lights, and camera. Keep
   object/material names meaningful and preserve source references.
5. Render, independently review the actual subject-specific gaps, revise within
   the stated cap, and save the accepted result. Do not polish unknown geometry
   into falsely precise claims.
6. Verify the requested deliverable. A still image establishes only that view;
   check animation continuously and runtime exports in their destination.

## Characters and companions

Use the bundled production recipe for soft surfaces. Preserve the subject's
identity anchors and expressive anatomy. Fur is optional when the material does
not call for it. Separate groomed source quality from rigs, eyelid deformation,
gaze control, animation, and runtime optimization. Review front, three-quarter,
face close-up, and any exposed attachment or back geometry. For chat use, also
inspect at the intended small size so facial features remain readable.

## Product visualization and commercials

- Extract silhouette, relative dimensions, label artwork, logo position, cap,
  seams, bevels, and material zones from actual references. Obtain dimensions or
  state approximation; one image does not prove the unseen back.
- Block out dimensionally, then refine bevel radii, thickness, joins, and
  silhouette. Use exact supplied label artwork and correct UV placement; do not
  generate replacement text or invent product features.
- Separate metal, plastic, glass, paper, liquid, and rubber materials according
  to evidence. Use large reflection cards/area lights to shape highlights. Check
  glass thickness/refraction and liquid level when those surfaces matter.
- Review front label readability, three-quarter silhouette, macro seams, and
  underside/back when visible in the requested shot. Check intersections and
  implausible reflections before increasing resolution.
- For turntables or reveal films, keyframe object/camera/light changes as needed;
  verify full motion, stable label projection, frame count, timing, and export.
  Reuse existing editing/audio workflows for a requested finished ad.

## Real estate, interiors, and environments

- Treat floor plans, site surveys, dimensions, and reference photos as different
  evidence sources. Set units. Bind room sizes, wall thickness, door/window
  openings, ceiling height, and furniture scale to known data.
- Without measured inputs, deliver a clearly identified conceptual scene. Do
  not invent an accurate floor plan, panoramic reconstruction, location view,
  structural specification, or existing amenity from a single image.
- Build a clean blockout and compare top/elevation views before furnishing.
  Inspect circulation, plausible openings, wall joins, and prop scale.
- Use material tiling at plausible real scale. Balance exterior illumination,
  window light, and practical lights; avoid solving every exposure problem with
  stronger emission or an artificially wide camera lens.
- Review eye-level interior/exterior views and details. For walkthroughs, check
  camera clearance, clipping, speed, transitions, flicker, and full sequence
  playback. Preserve an editable master with organized room/asset collections.

## Game props, characters, and environments

- Resolve the target engine, platform, art style, unit scale, and intended asset
  use. For a concept render only, do not add a full export pipeline unnecessarily.
  For a runtime asset, define measured geometry/texture/memory/frame-time budgets
  from the project rather than inventing universal polygon limits.
- Separate the high-quality source from the runtime mesh. Inspect topology,
  normals, silhouette at distance, UV layout, texel consistency, materials,
  origins/pivots, and transform scale. Bake detail where the target requires it.
- Create LODs, collisions, sockets, lightmap UVs, and rig/animation only where
  required by the destination. Groom curves may need a different representation;
  do not assume GLB or the engine reproduces Cycles hair and shaders.
- Export an agreed format, reimport it into the target tool/engine, and check
  orientation, size, texture links, deformation, lighting, and actual performance
  on the intended device. If destination testing is unavailable, mark runtime
  readiness unverified and deliver the source plus explicit export limits.
- Building a playable game adds interaction, controls, gameplay, and testing
  beyond producing the Blender assets. Keep those outcomes separate.

## Gaussian splat / reconstruction requests

Determine whether the user needs an editable mesh, a viewable captured scene,
animated geometry, or a render. These are different outputs. Inspect the actual
source capture/multi-view consistency and destination capabilities. Validate
training tools and hardware before committing to a run; any paid service follows
the user's cost approval rules. Never substitute camera orbit for character
animation or call an unmeasured splat an editable engineering/game mesh.
