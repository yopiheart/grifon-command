# Oceanides organic Reels production

Saved 2026-11-07 from Neil's approved illustrative-explainer pilot. This is a
client production workflow, separate from the Director wizard and the automated
static-image workflow in [oceanides_daily_posts.md](oceanides_daily_posts.md).
It does not change application prompts, presets, or publishing permissions.

## Objective and evidence

Build awareness and recall among residential and business customers who may
need Oceanides. Multiple future content pages share Oceanides branding; their
formats can differ. First validate individual episodes before multiplying pages.
Views help diagnose distribution, but qualified local inquiries and attributable
bookings are the business outcome. The pilot has no published performance data.

Before writing, separate three things:

- **Observed:** actual job footage, documented symptoms, crew findings, permission
  to use a customer's material, and recorded outcomes.
- **Inferred:** a possible explanation that the evidence does not establish.
  Keep the uncertainty in the wording and have the crew check it.
- **Illustrated:** invented scenes and explanatory animation. These can teach a
  concept; they cannot become customer testimony or proof that a job happened.

The current bathroom scene is illustrative. A toilet and floor drain sharing a
line do not establish a real customer's fault, a full septic tank, or the required
service. Crew review of the depicted drainage arrangement remains pending.

## Repeatable sequence

1. Choose one viewer, one recognizable problem, and one useful takeaway. Name
   the awareness stage and evidence available before selecting tools.
2. Choose the **format** (explainer, investigation, documented case, routine,
   interactive story) separately from its **style** (lighting, animation,
   typography, camera, rhythm, sound). A page is a distribution destination.
3. Inspect a specific reference Reel, including the opening, changes in viewer
   knowledge, motion, transitions, sound, and ending. Record timestamps and the
   limits of observation. High displayed views alone do not establish why it won
   or that it will bring Oceanides customers.
   For each format, map the reference's shot sizes, character or object motion,
   camera movement, cuts, inserts, text timing and ending before drawing the
   first frame. Use the chosen Reel to set the edit. Use the YouTube learnings
   to sharpen the hook, cause and consequence, and payoff within that edit.
   Compare the silent proof against the reference's actual beats before sound.
   Keep the Oceanides identity and readable safe margins across treatments.
4. Write a short beat sheet: time, viewer question/emotion, visual change,
   narration or text, evidence, and sound cue. Each beat should advance the
   explanation or story. Write exact customer-facing Taglish for Neil's approval.
5. Pick the medium by what the shot needs. Code suits precise typography,
   diagrams, and compositing. Blender suits an editable spatial explanation.
   Real footage suits real jobs and proof. AI images/video are options when the
   required scene benefits from them; they are not a prerequisite for quality.
6. Make a concept frame and a short motion proof before extending an expensive
   scene. Preserve the accepted source and render at each approval milestone.
7. Assemble the shortest clear cut. Use a connected visual change around every
   2-3 seconds as Neil's starting preference: a reveal, detail, motion, or reframing.
   Keep a readable hold when the information needs it. Random cuts and perpetual
   zoom are not substitutes for progression.
8. Add explanatory motion graphics. Draw onto the relevant geometry, select the
   subject with a callout, reveal a hidden layer, or animate a meaningful state
   change. Decorative camera movement alone did not meet this pilot's bar.
9. Check picture, claims, typography, logo, contact details, and mobile layout.
   Lock an approved picture version before its final sound pass.
10. Follow [background_music_production.md](background_music_production.md).
    Paid music, SFX, VO, images, or video require an exact approved test spec:
    purpose, inputs, settings, pass/fail criteria, evidence, and maximum cost.
    Historical approval is not permission to generate the next episode.
    Include any ElevenLabs library voice owner fee in the VO cap. Saveo's
    Pochology take incurred a separate 50-credit fee despite a composer with
    no multiplier notice. Reconcile the balance after each paid action before
    proceeding when the remaining budget is tight.
11. Sync the audible attack of each cue to the visual event, mix for intelligibility,
    inspect encoded frames, decode the whole export, and check stereo and mono.
    Review on a phone. Mark listening or platform checks pending if not observed.
12. Save editable inputs, native audio, spec, receipt, and verification evidence.
    Publish only on explicit instruction. After posting, record the post/page,
    observation window, retention, saves/shares, qualified inquiries, and bookings.
    Compare episodes within a similar format and audience; revise one major
    variable at a time when practical. Organic comparisons are directional, not
    randomized causal tests.

## Storytelling and voiceover

The new input is Neil's summary of the Dr. Plants / Think Media interview,
[episode 562](https://www.buzzsprout.com/391945/episodes/20069769-562-he-got-6-million-subscribers-with-only-12-videos).
[The YouTube interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iYPcnhNNjW0) was located,
but its full video/transcript was not independently inspected in this save pass.
Do not treat the summary's channel metrics, view multipliers, or causal claims as
verified evidence for Oceanides.

The transferable hypothesis is to make viewers follow a subject through a change.
For an Oceanides story, plan recognition, curiosity, concern, discovery, and a useful
payoff. That is an adaptation, not a compulsory Dr. Plants emotional sequence.
Show each clue or consequence when it changes the viewer's understanding. Do not
invent a customer, danger, deadline, diagnosis, or successful repair to create stakes.

For a future narrated investigation, a **25-35 second draft** is a reasonable
starting test: recognizable symptom -> question -> visible clues -> supported
explanation -> useful next step. If it is fictional, frame it as an illustration.
If it is a real case, the finding and outcome must come from that case's evidence.
This is a proposed format, not an approved new film or a proven duration.

Choose VO by its job:

| Format | Starting choice | VO must add |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Current 14-second illustrative explainer | Preserve the accepted music/SFX cut | A new VO version would need a clear improvement over its readable visual explanation. |
| Investigation or story | Draft with narration | Question, context, causality, stakes, and payoff beyond naming visible objects. |
| Documented crew case | Real operator narration where available | What was actually observed, checked, found, and done. |
| Short visual loop or transformation | Decide after the reference breakdown | Use narration only if it contributes beyond the motion. |

The earlier decision that VO was optional applies to the current pilot. It is not
a rule against narration. Conversely, a creator using VO is not proof that VO alone
caused their performance. Avoid reading every overlay aloud or crowding out the
sound and the reveal just to fill the timeline.

Review three ways: visuals/text with all audio muted for comprehension; music/SFX
with VO muted for visual rhythm and sound design; and the full mix for the intended
story and clarity. The interview summary's remove-VO exercise keeps music/SFX
audible. A narrated story need not communicate every fact with VO removed.

Use a few individual target viewers for qualitative feedback: what did they think
was happening, where did they lose interest, and what did they remember? Do not
confuse a polite compliment or a handful of viewers with demonstrated retention.
For a 14-second Reel, inspect the opening immediately rather than mechanically
waiting ten seconds to judge the hook.

Do not copy three uploads per year, a thousand-idea sprint, or an absolute novelty
requirement into a local service business. Start with relevant, easy-to-understand,
credible ideas and a sustainable production cadence. Keep outlier research for
discovery, then inspect the story and test the adaptation. Views do not settle it.

Existing Swipe File context: P2 supports shadow/no-box captions across multiple
references; P4 records counterexamples to continuous zoom; P5's near-continuous VO
observation is not a rule for every format. These are scoped observations in
[viral_swipe_file.md](viral_swipe_file.md), not permission to alter tuned presets.
This single interview does not satisfy the three-independent-video corroboration
gate for promoting a new reusable editing preset.
