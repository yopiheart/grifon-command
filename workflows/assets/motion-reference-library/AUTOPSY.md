# Five motion films: observed mechanics and Grifon implications

## Method and confidence

Scope: all five user-supplied films, total 365.60 seconds. Reviewed 20 overview
sheets covering the duration at 1 fps and 15 detail sheets at 10 fps. Three
2-second motion windows per film were inspected, 30 seconds in total. Times
below are approximate sampled-video times, not frame-exact edit decisions.

**Observed** means visible in inspected frames. **Interpretation** explains
the likely storytelling function. **AE route** proposes how we could rebuild
the effect. It does not establish which software or rig the author used.
Font families, numeric focal lengths, exact easing curves and generation models
cannot be verified from these exports. Palette names are visual descriptions,
not sampled brand tokens.

Success contract: retain every supplied source, identify each film's structure,
show timestamped camera/framing and object evidence, and distinguish evidence
from reconstruction advice. Failure: generic 'premium/fast/cinematic' notes,
pretending every transition is a camera move, lost sources, or unverified audio
claims. Verification: matching source/copy hashes, decodable audio/video proxies,
full-duration sheets, dense transition strips and links back to evidence.

## K01 — Commas Studios · 35.55 seconds

[Watch](proxies/K01-five-years.mp4) · [0–19s overview](sheets/K01-01.jpg) ·
[20s–end](sheets/K01-02.jpg)

**Hook and visual system.** A compressed introduction with folded/strip-like
light shapes and a blue accent becomes a comma mark on black. White/black
surfaces alternate; blue haze and mountain imagery provide depth. Compact,
tightly spaced sans-serif headings accompany real-looking product screens.
The footage supports a product-tour reading; software authenticity is unverified.

| Time | Observed framing, objects and transition | Story function / interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4s | Intro strips, name, comma mark, then logo on black | Establish identity before demonstration |
| 4–6s | Extreme crop of phone top expands to reveal the device and feed against mountains | Detail-to-context reveal, not another title card |
| 6–9s | Phone/background give way to a standalone post card; framing moves to engagement controls; large soft-focus Grow text | Reuse one object to connect post, engage and growth ideas |
| 9–14s | Dark community UI, navigation close-up, course grid, larger course cards | Alternate context and feature inspection |
| 14–18s | Paywalled post over night landscape becomes readable, with daylight landscape and expanding card | Unlocking content gets a visible before/after |
| 18–26s | Light UI: feed, courses, lesson editor and curriculum close-up | More specific product proof after broad promise |
| 26–30s | Selling/community line, screen over grass, pale brand field and enlarged comma | Exit the demo into a brand motif |
| 30–35.55s | Commas Studios lockup on black, then dimming | Deliberate closing hold, unlike the middle's feature changes |

**Three inspected mechanics.**

1. [4–6s](sheets/K01-detail-4.jpg): the phone top occupies most of the image at
   4.0–4.4. The pullback becomes pronounced around 4.5–4.9 and continues more
   gently to 5.9. The same logo/phone anchors the reveal. AE route: parent a
   device and UI to one transform; separate background and foreground haze.
2. [6–8s](sheets/K01-detail-6.jpg): at about 6.1–6.3 the isolated post retains
   continuity while its surroundings change. Around 7.0–7.3, framing rapidly
   enlarges and shifts to the lower engagement row. The heart fills red near
   7.5. The movement has a destination: the interaction being explained.
3. [16–18s](sheets/K01-detail-16.jpg): night becomes day around 16.3–16.5, while
   the paywall gives way to the post body around 16.5–16.8. The card remains
   the visual anchor and grows. AE route: background crossfade plus masked
   content reveal and a shared card transform; no video model required for UI.

**Inventory:** phone shell, post, reactions, community/course UI, thumbnails,
cursor, comma mark, environmental plates and haze. People appear inside
thumbnails; this is not evidence that the film needs a presenter.

**Take / avoid:** borrow detail-to-context framing and action-result continuity.
Do not borrow blue mountains merely because they look premium. Dense course
screens would need larger, verified Grifon details for small-screen legibility.
Audio: local ASR produced generic unsupported speech; that output is rejected
as transcript evidence. It cannot establish whether the soundtrack has VO.

**Three inspected mechanics.**

1. [8–10s](sheets/K02-detail-8.jpg): an angled screen holds through about 8.7;
   from 8.8–9.2 a green scan and visibly bent/distorted screen accompany a
   push toward the timeline. Perspective alone does not explain the bend.

   or mesh-warp treatment. Do not call this a recovered camera rig.

   cannot tell whether it used 2D scale or a true 3D camera.

**Take / avoid:** use one recognizable motif to connect steps. Avoid importing
the green orb, lengthy feature list or speculative sound-performance claims
into Grifon. Footage and environmental imagery are assets; they do not prove
that a generative video model was used or is necessary.
Audio: ASR supports a narrated product-demo structure; its duplicated section
around 21–30s and mistaken words make it unsuitable as a verbatim script.
The visible sound labels describe the product UI, not verified audible SFX.

## Synthesis: why the first Grifon study felt basic

The previous study relied on a text entrance, a hold and a scene replacement.
These references often preserve an object, turn it into the next object, or
move the viewer to a detail before revealing the result. Adding an AE camera
to title cards does not by itself close that gap.

| Observed principle | Evidence | Proposed Grifon application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Move attention toward an action | K01 7.0–7.5; K05 10.7–11.0 and 14.6–14.9 | Frame one real control or selected sentence, then show its consequence |
| Keep an object through a transition | K01 16–18; K03 18–20; K04 3.4–3.7 | An idea/selection evolves into script, then storyboard; preserve a visual anchor |
| Let the next composition assemble early | K02 17–19; K04 22.6–23.8 | Outgoing text and incoming product detail briefly share the stage |
| Depth has multiple contributors | K02 8–10; K03 75–77; K05 31–33 | Separate foreground/UI/background; use perspective only where useful |
| Speed is contrast, not constant acceleration | K01 rapid 7.0–7.3 focus vs 31–34 lockup; K02 35–50 demo; K05 31.7–32.0 settle | Short transition, readable settle, meaningful change, deliberate brand hold |
| Graphics carry meaning beyond captions | K03 spheres → circles; K04 button → next line | Design the semantic object first, then choose its motion |

These are observations across different product films credited to the same
creator, not independent performance corroboration. No existing Swipe File
pattern count or tuned prompt was changed. In particular, do not generalize
these into 'always zoom' or 'sound effect on every entrance.'

## Reconstruction routing for our AE workflow

- Editable words, cursor, chips, masks, selection boxes, paths and simple
  geometric morphs: native AE text/shape layers and keyframes.
- Real product evidence: verified Grifon screenshots/captures; split only the
  details that need independent motion. Do not invent functional UI as proof.
- Phone, paper, backgrounds or texture: supplied/licensed/generated stills
  when required by the shot. Separate foregrounds before animation.
- Perspective and spatial travel: 2.5D planes plus camera/parent controls.
  Camera movement and object movement must each have a storytelling purpose.
- Actual surface rotation, complex liquid/cloth or live action: a suitable
  existing clip, 3D render or separately approved generation if needed. A
  generative video model is not necessary for the demonstrated text/UI mechanics.
- Audio: separate voice, music and spot-effect stems, timed against the same
  beat map; verify the mix by listening before claiming completion.

## Audio evidence and limits

All five originals and proxies contain audio. Local CPU ASR drafts are retained
as optional navigation aids, not ground truth. K01, K03 and K05 produced generic
or repetitive unsupported text and are **rejected as transcript evidence**.
K02 has a duplicated clause and mistranscription. K04 yields a coherent
listing-to-matching-to-sale narrative consistent with the visual sequence;
its exact words and timestamps still need an auditory check. These defects demonstrate
why ASR alone cannot verify narration, music, beat sync or SFX quality.

This pass does not claim a verified BPM, exact sound library, exact sound cue
list, musical genre or complete listening review. Descriptive labels visible
inside Lana's UI are not evidence that those sounds occur in the soundtrack.
The retained audio allows a later listening pass without losing the sources.
See [verification.json](evidence/verification.json) for full decode, duration,
hash and measured audio-level checks. These measurements do not replace listening.

## What is now reusable, and what is not yet proven

Reusable now: source catalog, preserved viewing copies, camera/framing maps,
object inventory, timestamped transition evidence, reconstruction routes and
the explicit evidence/interpretation distinction. They form an input reference
for the future motion skill; the documents do not automatically train a model.

Not yet proven: a general-purpose MCP/AE integration, automated reference
matching, exact recreation fidelity, production audio quality, or a finished
Grifon film at this benchmark. The current camera pilot remains a study.
Before the next film revision, choose one compact product action and benchmark
its continuity against K04/K05; review moving playback, not only hero stills.
