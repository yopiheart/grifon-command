## Our proposed application — not Kendel's recovered SOP

| Stage | Reviewable material | What a useful review would establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Idea | Audience, single takeaway, truthful product benefit and visual premise | The viewer can understand what Grifon helps them do; the concept is more specific than attractive words |
| Script | On-screen copy, optional narration, product evidence and a timed beat map | Each beat advances the story; claims have support; words fit the available reading time; VO adds meaning |
| Storyboard | Key compositions, object continuity, framing start/end, transition destination, asset list and rough animatic | Movement is motivated; the viewer knows where to look; screenshots prove the point; pacing works before expensive polish |
| Final edit | AE playback and export with the actual voice/music/effects mix | Timing, easing, overlap, legibility and sound sync survive continuous playback; editable project and collected assets reopen correctly |

An animatic is a rough timed sequence used to assess rhythm; finished style
frames alone cannot establish motion quality. The camera can remain still when
object choreography communicates the beat. More movement is not automatically
a better review outcome.

These reviews are production quality checks. They do not imply asking Neil for
permission at every routine step. User choices are needed when the message or
scope is materially ambiguous; existing specific paid-operation and tuned-diff
approval rules still apply.

## Consequence for the current pilot

The existing AE study proves scripting, editable text and camera execution.
It does not prove a strong product story, a reviewed animatic or a final audio
mix. Its basic quality should not be addressed only by adding effects.

Before another polished 30-second render, the next reviewable deliverable is
a concise Grifon story with timed compositions and transitions, informed by
K04/Button and K05/SourceReady. That proposal should show what the viewer learns
and how each object/action leads to the next. This note records the production
lesson; it does not implement a new skill, train a model or reveal Kendel's SOP.
