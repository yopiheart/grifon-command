# Dr. Plants: ant-colony story and editing study

Observed 2026-11-07 for Oceanides organic production, using the viral-autopsy
workflow. Source: [Simulating an Ant Colony for 1000 Days](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PyiCHWfBQPY).
The official channel displayed 99M views; the watch page displayed about 566K
likes. These are dated reach signals, not evidence that a particular edit caused
performance. No account-baseline outlier ratio or audience retention was available.

No paid analyzer was run. Direct listening was not available in this pass.

## What holds the story together

The protagonist is established immediately, along with a time-bound goal and an
opponent. Subsequent facts explain a need, an obstacle, an attempted solution,
or the consequences. The main conflict stays unresolved while smaller questions
receive answers. The audience repeatedly gains something before being asked to
wait again. This is an interpretation of the structure, not measured psychology.

confirmed diagnosis, a customer's thoughts, or an actual service outcome.

Hard cuts and connected reframing are visible in the inspected sequences. The
distinctive transition is the movement between surface and chambers. There is no
basis to claim that every change uses a fancy transition, a measured zoom speed,
or physical camera motion rather than a crop/composite. Several macro frames
hold their composition while the animal supplies movement. Opening pet inserts
also change rapidly within one second. The rhythm is variable.

These windows bracket sampled states; they are not millisecond measurements of
the first/last text pixels. The creator's intention is inferred from the text,
composition, and adjacent action. There is no evidence every placement is perfect.
The faint underground label and the plant overlap are useful counterexamples.

For Oceanides, retain Neil's center-aligned top/middle/lower-middle white-text
direction and protect the subject and Reels controls. Borrow the **purpose and
timing** of these devices; do not copy landscape corner coordinates into 9:16.
Use a label when a location/state needs identification, a short emphasis when
its meaning lands, a pointer when the relevant detail is hard to find, and a
clear contact hold for action. Remove or reposition text when the next shot
supplies the answer, the subject enters its area, or a new beat replaces its job.

## What this adds to the Oceanides workflow

1. **Write a causal chain before polishing effects.** Name the subject, goal,
   obstacle, attempt, change, and supported payoff. Existing beat-sheet planning
   can hold this; no new harness is needed. Target: the brief upstream of the
   pilot's `motion/beats.json`, not a tuned application prompt.
2. **Plan an establishing view and a detail view for every important discovery.**
   Keep the same geography and return to it. Target: Blender camera plan and
   original footage shot list; `motion.mjs`/`render.mjs` only implement an authored
   decision after approval, not an automatic zoom on every scene.
3. **Give each overlay an explicit job and an exit.** Record its trigger,
   occupied area, reading time, subject collision, and reason to leave. The pilot's
   HTML/JSON renderer already supports timed centered plates; the lesson is in
   better planning, not adding a new text engine.
4. **Use narration to connect causes and consequences.** Avoid naming everything
   already obvious on screen. Preserve the music/SFX-only pilot; a narrated story
   is a separate proposed test. Actual delivery and sound design need listening
   before specifying a voice or musical treatment from this reference.
5. **Separate narrative interpretation from proof.** A fictional explainer may
   illustrate a possibility. A case study's discovery, timing, repair, and result
   need records from the real job. This matters more than importing an ant-war arc.

Keep the previously agreed concise copy, intentional animation, connected visual
changes, transparent updated logo, and readable contact details. This study adds
story and overlay decision-making; it does not replace them. Do not stretch one
service symptom into eight minutes, manufacture a disaster, reuse the creator's
footage/music in customer content, or equate broad nature-channel reach with local
service demand. Glovio remains the next reference after this study.
