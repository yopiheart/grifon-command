The first section appears largely locked and eye-level, based on the stable
tile lines and counter. The character turns and dips inside the frame. The
last scene changes scale and lighting. Samples show no added hook, step
labels, captions or CTA. Visible labels sit on the physical packaging,
including the opening tube. Source art is stylized and doll-like. The
software, generation method and exact camera mechanism are unverified.
The frame 375/376 pair verifies the finished-face jump. That boundary did
not clear the 0.18 scene-score cutoff, an example of why the scene scan
alone misses same-framing edits.

Audio exists and decoded. Offline Whisper base Q5_1 did not recover usable
spoken narration. Its language guess is weak evidence for content, so
direct listening is still needed before identifying music or individual
effects. FFmpeg measured -15.9 LUFS integrated and detected no interval
below -40 dBFS for at least 0.15s. Those are signal measurements only.

## Oceanides application

Test a short fictional crew readiness routine. Open on the original
stylized Filipino crew member with a glove already in hand. Hold one
frontal, eye-level workbench angle while the glove goes on, a jacket zip
closes and a flashlight clicks. Time each short edit to a visible hand
contact. Hold the face for the reaction, then cut wide as the prepared
character steps out through a doorway. The change in framing reveals the
result of the routine. The premise makes no claim about response time or
a real job.

Keep expressive eyes and one consistent original character. Use a dark
matte navy work shirt with restrained amber details. Start with a 12–14s
picture proof with no dialogue needed to follow the actions. Add effects
at the actual glove, zip and flashlight contacts only after the picture
works. The Temi Reel supports a frontal routine and earned wide reveal
for this one pilot. It does not establish a global caption or shot-length rule.
