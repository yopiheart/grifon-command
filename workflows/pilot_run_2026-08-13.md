## Findings to file (none filed yet)

1. **`SCENE_CAP` is not wired to the producer path.** It is imported only by
   `components/ExpertMode/ScriptStudio.tsx`, `components/GenerateVideo/index.tsx` and
   `utils/scriptGuardrails.ts`. `services/producerOrchestrator.ts` just follows
   `scenes.length`, so the model decides ad length. This is why this run came back **10
   scenes** and why `apr_69e5adc6` came back **9** on 07-31 — that was recorded as a
   mystery. Margin exposure: one credit is metered per finished ad regardless of length,
   and the pricing doc calls the cap "the margin dial."
2. **`api/_lib/modelCosts.ts` has no price row for the two models we actually run.**
   `veo-3.1-lite-generate-preview` and `kling-v3` both fall through to
   `DEFAULT_PER_SECOND = 0.1`. The COGS telemetry is therefore logging a placeholder.
3. **`producer_runs.cost_usd` is `0` on every row, including the 07-31 run.** The column
   exists; nothing populates it. Combined with #2 this is why every cost conversation so
   far has been guesswork. Fixing #2 and #3 together makes the next run self-measuring.
4. **Colour-grade drift is at 3/3 corroboration → the gated TUNED proposal is unlocked.**
   Scenes 2 and 5 came back with a warm cast against the neutral rest; a regen fixed both.
   Root cause is that no shared grade anchor exists across scene generations, so each scene
   rolls independently and consistency is luck. ⚠️ Deterministic alternative that does NOT
   touch tuned prompts: we already ship 7 LUTs in `luts/` plus `tools/grade.mjs` — one LUT
   applied across all scenes at the render stage would fix it in the graph, not the prompt.
5. **No shot-variety rule on the `ugc`/Testimonial skeleton.** Scenes 5 and 6 read as the
   same framing and distance with only the action changed. The UGC Pro skeletons carry
   per-scene shot specs; the testimonial one does not. **1/3 corroboration.**
6. **`TEXT_LIMIT = 30 / 60s` is mis-sized for the producer's burst.** One ordinary 10-scene
   run trips our own per-user limiter and eats up to ~2 minutes in backoff (4/8/16/32/64s),
   flooding the console and hiding real errors. Same class as the already-filed Kling
   "5/hr pool predates the producer's batch reality" finding.
7. **Face-distance / eye dead zone confirmed on the product.** Scenes 2 and 9 framed the
   presenter too far; scene 9's eyes visibly hallucinated. This matches the documented rule
   in `assets/house-model/README.md`: *"face big enough to stare at but too small to render
   properly = broken. Three-quarter body up, or genuinely far. Avoid the middle."*
   Neil's own split is the right one: **talking avatars must be close, b-roll may be far.**
   Both scenes were fixed by per-scene REGENERATE.

## Neil's open question worth more than the rest: the same-face problem

Auto-cast avatars keep landing on the same "mean face" per age band — the recognisable
social-media AI face that every nano-banana user gets. Already recorded as finding #3 of the
sample-batch spec. Neil's framing is the sharp one: **if our faces look like everyone
else's, the believability formula buys us nothing competitively.**

Two levers, and they are not equal:

- **Cheap and copyable:** the avatar brief only carries age / gender / ethnicity / market —
  four buckets with millions of people in each, so the model picks the average.
  `assets/house-model/README.md` already found the fix: **"Bone, not features"** — specify
  cheekbone height and width, jawline, neck length, eye spacing, philtrum, facial fat.
  Its own note: *"the first batch was believable but ordinary because I specified features
  and not structure."* ⚠️ Must be A/B'd **alone** on Gemini — the full raw-ugc scaffold was
  tested on `gemini-3.1-flash-lite-image` on 2026-07-16 and Neil failed all 4 pairs
  ("panget lahat"), so do not port the whole thing.
- **The actual moat:** stop letting the model choose. Extend the existing locked house model
  into a **casting bank of 15–20 structurally distinct, Neil-approved Filipino faces** and
  cast from it. That gives variety, per-ad consistency, and something a competitor cannot
  reproduce from a prompt, because it is an asset and not a prompt. The third lane —
  user-uploaded presenter — is already live and is the least copyable of all.
